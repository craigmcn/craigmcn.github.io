There was a time when a ringing phone meant one thing: someone wanted to talk to you, so you talked to them. That's not true anymore, and pretending otherwise is how you end up staring at a screen while it buzzes, waiting for it to stop. A call now arrives with no context, no subject line and no way to triage it before answering, which is exactly the problem. We've spent fifteen years building tools that let us decide when and how to respond to things, and then the phone still rings like it's 1995, expecting an answer on its own schedule rather than yours.

I push unsolicited calls to voicemail almost by default, and I don't think that's rude so much as reasonable. What I do expect, though, is that the caller leaves something behind. A voicemail with actual content tells me what the call was about, which means I can decide whether it needs a call back, an email or nothing at all. A missed call with silence after it does the opposite: it hands me the guessing and the anxiety that comes with it, and now I'm the one who has to initiate contact to find out what I missed in the first place.

This is where the "voicemail is dead" story gets it backwards. Voicemail isn't dying because nobody wants messages anymore. It's dying because the format stopped matching how people actually process information: scannable, skimmable, on their own timeline. The fix already exists and most people ignore it. Voicemail-to-text turns a medium built entirely around tone and pacing into something you can read in three seconds, which is precisely what makes it usable again. I'd rather get a transcript than a missed call notification with nothing attached.

None of this is a complaint about phones themselves. It's a complaint about unfinished handoffs, the missed call that assumes you'll chase it, the voicemail nobody thinks to leave, the expectation that live availability is still the default rather than the exception. The tools to fix this already exist. We've just collectively decided not to use them properly, and then act surprised when nobody picks up.
